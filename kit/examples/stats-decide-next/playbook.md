# Playbook: how the numbers are read

This is the method the analyst follows. It is written for short videos; the same structure works for posts or emails with your own measures. Revise the numbers once you have about twenty items of your own data.

## What is measured, in order of weight

1. **Shares per 1,000 views.** The strongest signal that a stranger cared.
2. **Watch quality.** The 3-second hold (the share of viewers still watching after three seconds), then the average watch time as a share of the reel's length. Below about 30% watched, the content barely matters: the opening failed.
3. **Saves per 1,000 views.** The "useful" signal.
4. **Follows.** Did a stranger care about you, not just the joke.
5. **The real outcome.** Clicks on your link, sign-ups, replies. Views are a means.

Raw views and likes are logged but decide nothing: views depend on the hook, and likes are cheap.

> **While you are small, the order changes.** My first ten trial reels got between about 10 and 160 views each, and every one had no shares and no saves, so shares could not rank anything. Until shares show up, the **3-second hold** leads, then the watched share. Shares and saves still win outright the moment an item has any. Go back to shares first once items routinely pass about 1,000 views.

## When the numbers are read

- **24 hours:** the decision point. On my first trials, views and hold did not move after that.
- **48 hours:** only a check that nothing late happened.
- **7 days:** the final numbers, for the record.

Always compare an item with **your own median** for the same window, not with internet benchmarks. Until you have enough items with 24-hour numbers there is no median: post, log, do not judge. With about 100 views an item, a hold rate is only good to about plus or minus 8 points, so treat a single verdict as provisional and lean on calls about a whole format (three items).

## Verdicts

Effort goes to winners. A slot spent re-cutting an average item is a slot not spent testing a new one.

| Verdict | When | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| `double` | Clearly best on the leading measures | Two or three variants within a few days |
| `keep` | Neither | Nothing. Note what worked in the opening, if anything |
| `kill` | Clearly worst on the leading measures | No re-cuts, no variants. Move on |

Per format, twice a week:

- **Drop a format** after three items of it all land below your median on the leading measures.
- **Double down on a format** when three or more of its items beat your median.
- **Keep about 20% of slots for new or losing formats**, so you do not over-fit on two weeks of data.

## How to double down

From one winner, change **one thing** at a time, so you learn what made it win:

1. **Same body, new hook:** was it the idea or the opening?
2. **Same hook and format, new topic:** does the format travel?
3. **Same idea, other format:** how far does the idea go?
4. **A part two**, within a week, while the audience exists.

## Things to watch

- If several items in a row get almost no views, suspect the account before the items.
- If your medians fall as you post more, the platform is splitting your reach: step back.
- One item with almost no distribution says little. Do not drop a format on it alone.
