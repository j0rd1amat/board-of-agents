A Sunday newspaper about my friends, written by agents

During the week, agents read my WhatsApp, LinkedIn, Instagram and Gmail so I don't have to scroll. They only read, and they save facts, each with the exact words it came from. Every Sunday at 9:00 another agent turns the week into a three-page newspaper on my laptop: who is moving, who is hiring, who has news, and who I should call.

  • 3pages every Sunday at 9:00
  • 30words at most in the quote behind every fact
  • 21days ahead for the events in each edition

How it works

Step 1

Readers scan one source at a time

Morning and evening, an agent reads each source and saves only what is new since the last scan: who said what, where, and the exact words.

Step 2

Every fact goes into a database on my laptop

One local file. Nothing about my friends is written to any online service, and only something that cannot wait until Sunday is worth a notification.

Step 3

On Sunday, the editor gathers the week

Facts first seen in the last seven days, events in the next 21, my corrections, and the people I have not talked to for longest.

Step 4

It writes and prints the paper

A front page with one lead story and two smaller ones, then Family, Friends, Network moves and Ideas in your feed. A script turns it into a PDF.

Step 5

It ends with who to call

Five to eight people I have not talked to in a while, each with a conversation starter taken only from something they said. Without one, it suggests a plain check-in.

The rules it runs under

  • Read-onlyThe agents never like, comment, follow, message or post, and they do not open pages that tell the other person I looked.
  • No quote, no factEvery fact carries the words it came from and who wrote them. My first edition invented a meet-up nobody had proposed; that is why this rule exists.
  • It stays on my laptopHealth, religion, politics and money are left out unless the person shared a milestone themselves. One person's news is never passed to anyone else.
  • My corrections winWhen I mark a fact wrong, it never comes back, reworded or not.

   - `quote`: the verbatim words, at most 30 words
   - `speaker`: who actually wrote them
   - `chat`: the chat or post it came from
   - `status`: `info`, `open` or `resolved`
